# Slop Copter: unexpected moments and escalation

Ideas saved for later review. These are possibilities, not a committed implementation plan.

## Direction

Start with a familiar, nostalgic helicopter-and-carriage game. Each failure
changes something. The green digital world emerges, and eventually the stuntmen
get up and come after the player. Precision landings grow into flying, dodging,
fighting, and discovering increasingly absurd surprises within the familiar scene.

The surprises gradually change what the player thinks the game is about:
first a landing game, then a rebellion, then an escape from something much bigger.

## Unexpected moments

1. **The first survivor remembers you.**
   A missed stuntman stands up, dusts himself off, and silently watches the
   helicopter. Several rounds later, he returns wearing a helmet and carrying
   equipment.

2. **The carriage driver starts anticipating you.**
   He looks upward before you drop, then briefly brakes or accelerates.
   Eventually you discover you can bait him with a fake drop.

3. **The horse quits.**
   It unhooks itself and walks away. The driver pushes the carriage by hand.
   Later, the horse returns driving something much faster.

4. **A stuntman refuses to let go.**
   Pressing Drop makes him shake his head. You have to shake him loose—or
   discover that carrying him through the next obstacle earns his trust.

5. **Someone catches the falling man.**
   An angry stuntman on the ground unexpectedly saves another. They shake
   hands, look up at you, and begin working together.

6. **The cloud contains a room.**
   Fly into it and the cloud becomes transparent, revealing people operating
   the weather. One of them notices you watching and closes the curtain.

7. **The hay is alive.**
   It shuffles toward falling stuntmen, then grows little legs. Eventually
   the carriage is chasing its own haystack.

8. **Your first rescue changes allegiance.**
   Catch a falling enemy with the helicopter's skid. He climbs aboard and
   starts repairing damage—or shooting at his former friends.

9. **The crew steals your spare helicopter.**
   A rival aircraft enters using the same controls and acceleration you have.
   Its pilot is a stuntman you recognize.

10. **The carriage becomes a trampoline.**
    A perfect landing launches the stuntman back upward. Suddenly you're
    catching him, dropping him again, and building chains between moving targets.

11. **Something huge passes behind the scenery.**
    You see only a shadow or an enormous leg. Several rounds later, that
    creature becomes the terrain you're flying across.

12. **The screen edge stops wrapping normally.**
    You emerge from the opposite side with a tiny delay. Then another
    helicopter emerges behind you: a replay of your previous flight.

13. **Your previous attempt becomes part of the level.**
    A ghost follows the exact route you flew last round. It can catch jumpers,
    block shots, or become a moving platform. You're accidentally designing
    your next challenge.

14. **The helicopter gets boarded.**
    An enemy lands on the rotor housing and starts crawling toward the
    cockpit. Banking shakes him loose; flying under a cloud might knock him off.

15. **The digital rain becomes physical.**
    Falling symbols collect into platforms, shields, and obstacles. Your rotor
    can scatter them, opening temporary paths.

16. **A stuntman lands on the score.**
    The score display bends under his weight. He pulls down a digit and uses
    it as a shield. Eventually the HUD becomes part of the playfield.

17. **The ground folds upward.**
    The carriage continues driving up the wall and across the ceiling. Its
    wheels stay attached, but your stuntmen still fall downward—until you
    find a gravity switch.

18. **The camera finally follows you.**
    You fly beyond what seemed like the edge of the game and discover the
    original playfield was one small area in a sprawling world.

19. **There are other stunt shows nearby.**
    Adjacent areas contain different versions of the game: a circus, a
    military test range, an underwater copy. Their characters begin crossing
    into your level.

20. **A failed landing punches through the world.**
    The stuntman falls through a crack in the ground. You can follow him
    into the machinery beneath the simulation, where conveyors physically
    move the scenery above.

21. **You briefly become the falling stuntman.**
    A clearly signaled special event switches the camera to his perspective.
    Steer toward a landing, grab a passing helicopter, or board the carriage
    to take control of it.

22. **The carriage launches into flight.**
    The driver unfolds wings. You're now landing stuntmen on an aircraft
    that banks, dives, and occasionally flies upside down.

23. **The enemy builds a boss from your wreckage.**
    Crashed helicopters, abandoned carriages, and broken scenery assemble
    into a machine. Its weapons reflect the things you lost earlier in the run.

24. **An enemy asks for help.**
    Something worse arrives. The people who were shooting at you start
    signaling for evacuation. You have to fly through the battle collecting
    your former attackers.

25. **The apparent final boss is holding the world together.**
    Destroying it causes the ground, clouds, and HUD to detach. The next
    sequence is a frantic escape through the collapsing pieces.

26. **You fly out of the arcade cabinet.**
    The camera pulls back and reveals the whole game running on a machine
    in another pixel-art world. Your helicopter breaks through its screen
    and keeps flying.

27. **The original carriage is still coming.**
    Much later, during an enormous battle, that familiar horse and carriage
    roll into view. Landing one stuntman in the hay becomes the surprisingly
    emotional way to finish the sequence.

28. **The crew eventually puts you on the hook.**
    A stuntman takes the pilot's seat. Your character is now hanging beneath
    the helicopter, waiting for someone else to decide when to drop.

## Keeping surprises fun across runs

- **Events react to your behavior.** Frequent crashes create salvage enemies.
  Perfect landings attract spectators. Rescuing people builds a crew.
- **Good play reveals surprises too.** Successful players should reach the
  strange stuff; misses can change its form or bring it forward.
- **Introduce an idea, let players master it, then combine it with another.**
  First the trampoline carriage. Later the flying carriage. Eventually both.
- **Give discoveries useful consequences.** Finding the weather room lets you
  redirect wind. Befriending the horse unlocks a rescue. Ghost flights become tools.
- **Include quiet moments.** After a huge battle, one surviving stuntman sitting
  on the carriage and waving can be more memorable than another explosion.

## A possible story progression

The survivor watches you → the crew organizes → you rescue an enemy → both
sides face something worse → you escape the original playfield together.

This gives the growing absurdity some characters worth caring about.
